The problem

I like switching between reading and listening, depending on whether I’m at my desk or in the car. Every time I switched, I lost my place and ended up scrubbing through hours of audio to find where I left off. The big platforms sync reading and listening, but only for books you buy in their store. Nothing did it for files I already owned, so I built it.

Decisions and guardrails

  • My own Google Drive as the backend. There’s no server to run, pay for or secure, and the data stays in my account. The app can only see files it created.
  • Local transcription. The books are copyrighted and private, so I ran Whisper on my PC instead of sending audio to a cloud service. It’s slower, but it’s free and nothing leaves the machine.
  • No “last save wins.” Every saved position is kept as history. If my phone and PC disagree, the app asks me which one is right. The farthest point isn’t always where I stopped.
  • Sized for one user. One PC manages the shared library and each phone keeps its own file. For a real product, I’d move to a proper sync service and cloud transcription.
  • There’s no analytics or tracking. The desktop app only accepts connections from the same computer. Google credentials and the app’s signing key are encrypted with Windows’ built-in protection.
